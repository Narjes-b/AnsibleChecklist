# Participant information and consent — DRAFT, not for recruitment

Study: Understanding Ansible static-analysis findings with source evidence and
optional AI explanations.

Research contact: **TO BE PROVIDED BY THE STUDY OWNER**.
Institution/review determination: **PENDING OWNER CONFIRMATION**.
Data-retention period, access arrangements and withdrawal deadline:
**TO BE PROVIDED BEFORE RECRUITMENT**.
Compensation, if any: **TO BE CONFIRMED BEFORE RECRUITMENT**.

You are invited because you are an adult developer with experience authoring or
reviewing Ansible code. Participation takes about 45 minutes. After a short
practice task, you review 12 findings and answer questions about the condition,
supporting evidence, and appropriate action. Some tasks include an AI explanation
which may be incorrect. Each task permits up to 150 seconds.

Participation is voluntary. You may decline, stop, or request withdrawal under
the finalized arrangements without penalty. Do not provide names, employer
information, confidential code, or real credentials in your responses.

The study records a pseudonymous participant ID, consent timestamp, years of
Ansible experience, task answers, confidence, elapsed time and timeouts. The
offline page sends no data over a network. You download a JSON response file and
return it through the private channel agreed with the facilitator. Any contact
information is kept separately from study responses.

The researchers will assess responses using independently reviewed rubrics and
compare study conditions. Research reporting should use aggregate results;
sharing anonymized individual responses or quotations requires the finalized
consent arrangements. There is no guaranteed personal benefit. Possible burdens
include time, task difficulty and frustration with incorrect AI suggestions.

Before participation, the facilitator supplies the completed contact, retention,
review and withdrawal information, answers questions, and confirms eligibility.
Consent is then recorded in the offline study page. This draft is not an ethics
approval or authorization to begin recruitment.
